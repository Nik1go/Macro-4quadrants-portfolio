## 1 · Sharpe and Sortino (app bug)

The app shows a negative Sharpe (−0.12) and Sortino (−0.19) for a portfolio with a positive 6.51% CAGR. With a 0% risk-free rate, recalculated from `trajectoires_fourmis.csv`, they should be about +0.63 and +0.89.

| Series | Sharpe (rf = 0%) | Sortino (MAR = 0%) | Shown in app |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ACO portfolio | 0.63 | 0.89 | −0.12 / −0.19 |
| CAC 40 | 0.18 | 0.25 | not shown |
| CAC Mid 60 | −0.11 | −0.15 | not shown |

To get −0.12 for the portfolio, the risk-free rate would need to be about 8% a year, which doesn't fit 2022–2026. The app's values sit close to the CAC Mid 60 row, so the function may receive the wrong series (a column index or dict key mix-up), or the risk-free rate may be in the wrong unit (percent vs decimal, annual vs daily).

Not checked: the Sharpe/Sortino code isn't in `core.py` or the notebook, so it lives in the Streamlit page file. The caption under the ratios also looks garbled (« taux sans risque Sortino : écarts sous 0 % … »): the risk-free rate value seems to be missing from the text, which points to the same function.

Formulas to use (annualised, 252 sessions, daily simple returns `r`, daily risk-free `rf_d = rf / 252`):

```
excess   = r - rf_d
Sharpe   = mean(excess) / std(excess, ddof=1) * sqrt(252)
downside = sqrt(mean(minimum(excess, 0) ** 2))      # over all sessions
Sortino  = mean(excess) / downside * sqrt(252)
```

Pick the risk-free rate on purpose and state it on the page: 0% is simple and matches the current caption; the average €STR over the period (roughly 2–3%, approximate) would be more realistic and give a portfolio Sharpe around 0.4.

## 4 · Method points to document (not bugs)

These aren't errors, but a professor could ask about them, so it's better to state them on the page.

- **The return floor isn't binding.** A swap-based search with no CAGR constraint lands on the same basket, which already returns 6.51% against a 1.66% floor. On this period the constraint never shapes the answer. A stricter floor (e.g. CAC 40 + 5%) would make it bite and show the constraint handling at work.
- **The colony fully converges by iteration 27 of 80.** At the end, the 10 chosen stocks sit at τ max = 5 and the 87 others at τ min = 0.05. New distinct baskets per iteration fall from 40 at the start to under 20 at the end, so about half the budget re-draws known baskets.
- **The global best can deposit twice.** `deposits = [global best] + elites`: when this iteration's best is also the global best, its stocks get the top two shares. A deliberate choice is fine, but say it is one.
- **Local optimality check.** No 1-for-1 or 2-for-2 swap lowers volatility while meeting the floor, and 6 other seeds (1, 2, 3, 7, 123, 2026) give the same basket. Good evidence, still not a proof.
